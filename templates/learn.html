⚡ The Basics — Dimensions

18m
Court Length
9m
Court Width
3
Max Touches / Possession

Volleyball is played on an 18m × 9m court — roughly the size of three parking spaces side by side. A net divides the court in half, and two teams of 6 players compete to send the ball over the net and land it on the opponent's side before it hits the ground.

Each team gets max 3 touches before the ball must cross back. The classic sequence is Pass → Set → Spike.

🏆 Scoring

25
Points to win a set
(2-pt lead required)
15
Points in 5th set
(if match reaches it)

A match is best of 5 sets. The first team to 25 points (with at least a 2-point lead) wins a set. If it reaches a deciding 5th set, that one goes to 15 points.

🔄 Rotation

Players rotate clockwise each time their team earns the right to serve. Everyone cycles through all 6 positions — front row attacks and blocks; back row passes and defends.

📸 THE SERVE IN ACTION

Watch the toss height, arm extension, and body position — all critical technique cues.

💪 3 Types of Serves

Every single point in volleyball begins with a serve. The server stands behind the end line and must send the ball over the net — without stepping over before making contact. You have just 8 seconds after the whistle.

🟢
Underhand
Hold the ball in front, swing arm forward from below, contact with closed fist. Easiest to control — but easiest to receive.
🟡
Overhand
Toss ~1 foot above head, extend hitting arm fully, drive open palm through the ball at peak of toss. Competitive standard.
🔴
Jump Serve
Approach like an attack, toss high, spike it airborne. Elite weapon — pros reach 120+ km/h. An unreturnable serve = an ace.

📸 THE FOREARM PASS

Notice the flat platform, locked elbows, bent knees, and weight forward.

🙌 Forming the Platform

The forearm pass — called a 'bump' — is the most important skill in volleyball. Bad passing teams never win. Your first job when the opponent serves: control the ball and deliver it cleanly to your setter.

Clasp hands together with thumbs parallel (not interlocked) and press forearms together. When the ball arrives, don't swing your arms — absorb and redirect by angling your platform and driving up through your legs.

Key mental model: Think of your forearms as a tilted table, not a baseball bat. Your legs do the lifting — arms barely move.

👟 Footwork & Targeting

Footwork is half the skill. Move your feet to get behind and under the ball before it arrives — never reach sideways for it.

🎯
Aim: 3m in the air near setter — 1m from net, right side
🛡️
Libero (different jersey) takes the hardest serves — best passer on the team

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ LESSON 4 — The Set ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {% elif lesson_num == 4 %}

📸 THE SET

Fingertips up above the forehead, wrists ready to extend — this is the moment the offense comes alive.

👆 The Setter's Role

After the pass, the setter takes the second touch — transforming an imperfect pass into a perfectly placed ball for the attacker. It's the most technical skill in volleyball.

Form a loose triangle above your forehead — thumbs and index fingers lightly touching — and contact with fingertips only. A quick wrist extension delivers the ball. If it rests in your hands even briefly, referees call a 'lift.'

⬆️
High outside set — gives hitter time, but lets blockers set up too
⚡
Quick middle set — one of the hardest plays to defend against
🔙
Back-set — ball delivered behind you without turning — keeps defense guessing

📸 THE SPIKE

Notice the full arm extension, open hand contact, and attacker's arm position at peak jump.

⚡ The Approach

The approach is as important as the jump. Right-handed hitters use a 3-step sequence — converting horizontal momentum into vertical explosion.

3-step for right-handed hitters: Left → Right-Left plant → Jump → Contact

At the peak of your jump, your hitting arm extends fully and your open hand contacts the ball — wrist snapping forward to drive the ball down at a steep angle. A spike that directly wins the rally = a kill.
